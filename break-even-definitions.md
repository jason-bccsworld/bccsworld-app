# BREAK-EVEN DEFINITIONS EXPLAINED
## Monthly vs. Cumulative Break-Even

---

# MONTHLY BREAK-EVEN

## Definition
**Monthly break-even** occurs when your **monthly revenue exceeds your monthly expenses** for the first time.

## BCCS142 Example
```
Month 16 Monthly Break-Even:
- Monthly Revenue: $250,000
- Monthly Burn Rate: $235,417
- Monthly Profit: $14,583
- Status: ✅ BREAK-EVEN ACHIEVED
```

## What This Means
- **Cash Flow**: You're generating more cash than you're spending each month
- **Operations**: Your business model is working
- **Sustainability**: You could theoretically run forever at this level
- **Investor Confidence**: Proves product-market fit

## Key Insight
You're **operationally profitable** but still need to **pay back initial investment**.

---

# CUMULATIVE BREAK-EVEN

## Definition
**Cumulative break-even** occurs when your **total revenue exceeds your total investment** since inception.

## BCCS142 Example
```
Month 36 Cumulative Break-Even:
- Total Revenue Collected: $17,250,000
- Total Investment Made: $12,930,000
- Net Position: $4,320,000 profit
- Status: ✅ BREAK-EVEN ACHIEVED
```

## What This Means
- **Investment Recovery**: You've paid back all investor money
- **True Profitability**: The business has generated more than it consumed
- **Exit Readiness**: Investors can see positive returns
- **Self-Sustaining**: No more external funding needed

## Key Insight
You've **fully recovered** all money invested and are **truly profitable**.

# KEY TAKEAWAY

## Monthly Break-Even = Business Model Validation
- **Month 16**: "We've proven this works"
- **Significance**: Sustainable operations
- **Investor View**: Reduced risk, proven concept

## Cumulative Break-Even = Investment Success
- **Month 36**: "We've made money for everyone"
- **Significance**: Complete investment recovery
- **Investor View**: Mission accomplished, exit ready

Both are important milestones, but they measure different things:
- **Monthly**: Can you sustain operations?
- **Cumulative**: Have you returned investor capital?

The gap between them (16 months to 36 months) represents the "investment recovery period" where you're operationally profitable but still paying back initial funding.
