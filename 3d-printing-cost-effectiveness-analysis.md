# 3D Printing Cost-Effectiveness Analysis: Supply Chain vs AOG Considerations

## 🎯 Executive Summary

The cost-effectiveness analysis reveals that **3D printing integration is highly justified** when compared to current supply chain costs and AOG expenses. The investment pays for itself through dramatic cost savings and operational improvements.

## 💰 Current Supply Chain & AOG Cost Reality

### **Aircraft on Ground (AOG) Costs**
- **$10,000-$150,000 per hour** depending on aircraft type and situation
- **Average $62 billion annually** global AOG cost burden across industry
- **$8,785 average spare parts cost** per AOG incident
- **14 AOG incidents per aircraft per year** average in US

### **Supply Chain Crisis Impact (2024)**
- **Material costs up 8.3%** (vs. historic 3-4% annually)
- **Labor costs up 7.3%** (vs. historic 2-3% annually)
- **Lead times extended**: Electrical components from 18 weeks to 30 weeks
- **MRO spending hit record $104 billion** in 2024

### **Inventory Carrying Costs**
- **25% of inventory value annually** in carrying costs
- **Major airlines**: $1.5-2B inventory turning <1.7 times/year
- **$275,000 inventory per helicopter** = $68,750/year carrying cost
- **Components**: Capital (financing), storage, insurance, obsolescence risk

## 📊 3D Printing ROI Analysis

### **Cost Savings vs. Traditional Supply Chain**

#### **Per-Part Cost Reduction**
- **30-50% cheaper** than OEM spare parts
- **Lead time reduction**: 12+ weeks → 2 weeks or days
- **Inventory elimination**: $0 carrying costs for digital files
- **Emergency response**: Hours vs. weeks for critical parts

#### **Real-World Example: Air New Zealand**
- **Traditional sourcing**: 44-day lead time, $30,000 revenue loss
- **3D printing**: Part delivered before aircraft landed
- **Savings**: $30,000 revenue loss avoided + faster turnaround
